    Why the law was enacted

    The Rizal Law (Republic Act 1425)

    Signed on June 12, 1956, the Rizal Law mandates all educational institutions in the Philippines to offer courses about Jose Rizal, particularly the study of his life, works, and writings.

    "It is the duty of every citizen to study the life and works of Jose Rizal, not merely to honor a national hero, but to draw from his example the strength and wisdom needed for nation-building."

    The law was authored by Senator Claro M. Recto, who believed that a deep understanding of Rizal's ideals would inspire patriotism among Filipino youth. The legislation faced opposition from the Catholic Church, which objected to the mandatory reading of Rizal's novels.

    Why does it matter today?

    The Rizal Course remains part of the college curriculum because Rizal's advocacy for education, critical thinking, and peaceful reform continues to resonate with contemporary challenges facing the Philippines.
